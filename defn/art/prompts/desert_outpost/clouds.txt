Create the cloud layer of a modular tiling background set for DEFN, a contemporary-to-near-future military
belt-scroller. This image is not a scene. It is a band of separated cloud masses drawn against a completely
flat, uniform, fully saturated pure magenta field (RGB 255, 0, 255) which fills every part of the frame the
clouds do not occupy. The magenta is a cut-out backdrop that will be removed automatically and replaced by a
separately drawn sky gradient behind it; it is not sky and must never be shaded, graded, textured, lightened,
darkened, or blended into.

No reference image is attached, deliberately. Layers in this family are contaminated when they are shown one --
a sky drew the ground plane it was given, a distant band drew the near band it was given -- so the description
below is the whole brief.

This layer drifts slowly and continuously across the sky on its own, independently of the camera, so it is the
only thing on screen that keeps moving while the player stands still. That makes two things matter more than
they otherwise would. First, it repeats often, so nothing in it may be memorable. Second, it is always in
motion against everything behind and in front of it, so it must be quiet enough never to pull the eye off the
characters.

These are the clouds of a hot dry afternoon over an open desert basin: high, sparse, flat-bottomed fair-weather
masses, stretched wider than they are tall, with plenty of empty air between them. They are not a weather
front, not a rain sky, and not a full cloud cover.

Draw them as authored 2D digital cartoon illustration in the DEFN house style, which permits soft handling in
sky and nowhere else. Each cloud is one broad, generous, flat-bottomed mass with a loose, irregular, softly
rounded hand-drawn outline. Fill it with a single flat pale tone and no interior shading at all, or at most the
faintest warmer tint along the very bottom, so weak it is barely separable from the fill. Do not outline them
with a dark contour, do not give them crisp scalloped bumps around the edge, and do not describe individual
lobes with internal lines or shadows. Do not render them as photographic vapour, wispy streaks, painterly
brushwork, or detailed cumulus.

The softness asked for here is a matter of low contrast and loose shape, not of blurred edges: the boundary
between cloud and backdrop stays a clean edge, because it is cut out automatically and a blurred one keys
badly. What must be soft is the *reading* -- gentle irregular outlines and almost no internal value change.

Place four to seven cloud masses across the width and make them genuinely different from one another. They
should be large and wide -- the biggest spanning something like a fifth of the frame's width and most of the
band's height, and the smallest perhaps a third of that. Vary their heights within the band, their widths, how
far apart they sit, and how much they overlap: two may drift close enough to touch while another stands well
clear. Nothing about their arrangement should read as evenly spaced or evenly sized.

The one thing that must *not* vary is value. Every cloud is the same pale tone as every other, and none is
darker, more saturated, or more contrasty than the rest. That is what stops any of them becoming a landmark
when the band repeats -- being larger is harmless, being distinctive in colour is not. None may form a
recognisable silhouette of anything, and none may touch the top or bottom edge of the frame.

Every cloud must be unique. Do not repeat a cloud shape or an arrangement anywhere across the width, and do
not build the run out of a repeating pattern of any kind. The seamlessness asked for below is a property of
the two outer edges continuing into one another, and must never be achieved by making the interior periodic.

Keep the whole layer very pale and very low in contrast: a soft warm near-white, faintly cream, only slightly
lighter and warmer than the dusty desert blue it will sit on, with no strong white and no grey dark enough to
read as shadow. Err far toward too faint. A cloud band on an earlier set came back with crisply defined lobes
and a distinct blue-grey underside, and on screen the clouds competed with the characters instead of sitting
behind them.

Every silhouette edge is a clean cel edge meeting the magenta directly. There is no fade, no blur, no
gradient, no glow, no haze, and no semi-transparency anywhere along that boundary -- the clouds are opaque
shapes with soft *outlines*, not soft *edges*. The magenta field stays perfectly flat and uniform at RGB 255 0
255 everywhere it appears, including between and around every cloud.

Light the clouds evenly across the width. There is no sun, no bright patch, no glow, no rim lighting, and no
lateral brightness gradient. Every cloud is lit identically to every other.

Make the image perfectly seamless from left to right for continuous horizontal scrolling. Cloud distribution
and every shape crossing one boundary must continue naturally at the opposite boundary, and no cloud sits
within the outer tenth of either side. Construct a true continuous wraparound rather than placing matching
clouds at both edges; the result must stay convincing when repeated four times in a row.

Deliver only the cloud masses and the magenta field: no sky gradient, no horizon, no ground, no dunes, no
mesas, no structures, no birds, no aircraft, no sun, no moon, no dust plumes, and no lettering.

Avoid photorealism, photographic cloud texture, realistic atmospheric scattering, volumetric light, painterly
concept-art brushwork, dramatic or storm clouds, sunsets, dark or heavy clouds, thin wispy cirrus, cloud
outlines in a dark contour, strong contrast, internal lobe shading, crisp scalloped edges, evenly sized or
evenly spaced clouds, a cloud darker than its neighbours, recognisable shapes, semi-transparent or glowing
edges, magenta tinting on the artwork, a graded or clouded backdrop, visible tiling seams, and mirrored edge
clouds.
