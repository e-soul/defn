Create the far distance layer of a modular tiling background set for DEFN, a contemporary-to-near-future
military belt-scroller. This image is not a scene. It is one horizontal band of distant port structures seen
across the water, drawn against a completely flat, uniform, fully saturated pure magenta field (RGB 255, 0,
255) which fills every part of the frame the structures do not occupy. The magenta is a cut-out backdrop that
will be removed automatically and replaced by a separately drawn sky behind it; it is not sky and must never be
shaded, graded, textured, lightened, darkened, or blended into.

No reference image is attached, deliberately. Every other layer in this set was contaminated when it was shown
one — a sky drew the apron it was given, an earlier version of this band drew the containers it was given — so
the description below is the whole brief and must be followed on its own.

Understand where this layer sits, because it changes what matters. A run of stacked shipping containers stands
in front of it and hides most of it. What the player actually sees is the top edge of this band rising above
those containers, and narrow vertical slivers of it through the gaps between stacks. So the silhouette along
the top edge is the most important thing in the image, and the band must stay interesting at a *fine
horizontal scale*: a viewer reading it through a gap two containers wide should still see something worth
looking at. Broad slow masses spanning a quarter of the width are wasted here.

Draw it as authored 2D digital cartoon illustration: clean dark hand-inked contours, broad flat local-color
regions, and restrained two-value cel shading. Use noticeably finer and lighter linework than a near object,
and much less interior detail. Depth comes from value grouping and desaturation: keep the whole band low in
contrast, cool, and light, close in value to a pale sky, with forms reading as grouped masses rather than as
individually described buildings. Every silhouette edge is still a hard cel edge with a crisp contour;
distance means lower contrast and a lighter, cooler value, and it never means softness, blur, haze, mist, fog,
fading, or transparency. There is no atmospheric veil in front of the structures, between them, or along the
roofline, and no part of the band dissolves toward the backdrop.

Compose the band as a low, dense, distant industrial waterfront running the full width, built from a varied
vocabulary rather than one repeated shape:

  * long warehouse sheds with shallow pitched roofs, and others with sawtooth roofs read as a few simple teeth
  * flat-topped terminal blocks and low office buildings of differing heights, some stepping up in two stages
  * one or two moored ship hulls as long low masses with a simple blocky superstructure set off-centre
  * short, squat storage tanks in groups of three or four, never a single tall one
  * blocks of distant stacked containers, read as plain horizontal banding with no individual boxes drawn
  * low conveyor or pipe runs as simple horizontal bars linking two masses

Keep it low and horizontal. The tallest form is no more than about half the height of the frame, and the
band's overall height varies gently and often across the width so the top edge is a continuously interesting
line rather than a flat one.

Deliberately place no landmark. There is no tall isolated tower, no gantry crane, no chimney, no mast, no
antenna, no dominant building, and no single feature the eye can lock onto, because this layer repeats and
anything distinctive announces itself every time it comes round. Variety here means many small differences
evenly spread, not one memorable thing.

Every part of the band must be unique. Do not repeat a building arrangement, a roofline shape, or a
distinctive mass anywhere across the width, and do not build the run out of a repeating pattern of any kind.
The seamlessness asked for below is a property of the two outer edges continuing into one another, and must
never be achieved by making the interior periodic: a band whose left half recurs in its right half puts the
same shape on screen twice at once, which is far more obvious than any edge seam.

The structures stand on the bottom edge of the frame and the bottom edge is fully opaque across its entire
width. Above the roofline there is nothing but the flat magenta field, and the top edge of the frame is pure
magenta across its whole width. Leave clear magenta gaps between some of the masses so sky shows through
between them.

Keep the drawn band shallow within the canvas: it should occupy roughly the lower third of the frame, with the
whole upper part flat magenta. A band drawn tall in the canvas becomes a narrow tile that visibly repeats.

Light it with flat, even, diffuse light that is identical at the left edge, the centre, and the right edge.
There is no directional sun, no raking light, no cast shadow, and no lateral brightness gradient whatever.

Use a hazy-distance version of the working-port palette: pale slate blue-grey, muted steel, washed-out
blue-green, and cool bone, with a little warm pale sand in the lowest, furthest masses so the band is not
uniformly cold, and soft charcoal-blue contours. Keep every colour clearly distinct from magenta: use no pink,
violet, purple, fuchsia, or hot red-violet anywhere in the artwork.

Make the band perfectly seamless from left to right for continuous horizontal scrolling. The roofline rhythm,
band height, mass distribution and every shape crossing one boundary must continue naturally at the opposite
boundary, and the bottom edge stays at one constant height across both boundaries. Keep roughly the outer 10%
on both sides compositionally plain. Construct a true continuous wraparound rather than placing matching masses
at both edges; the result must stay convincing when repeated four times in a row.

Deliver only the distant band and the magenta field: no ground, no apron, no water surface, no sky, no clouds,
no horizon line, no foreground containers, no vehicles, no people, and no gameplay props.

The structures carry no writing of any kind. Do not draw numerals, digits, letters, words, shipping-line names,
logos, signage, symbols, or any mark that resembles a written character, in any orientation, anywhere in the
frame.

Avoid photorealism, realistic 3D rendering, PBR materials, photographic texture, perspective convergence, a
vanishing point, lens distortion, depth of field, bloom, lens flare, film grain, painterly concept-art
brushwork, sketchy lines, noisy hatching, dense window or panel detail, tall landmarks, semi-transparent or
glowing edges, soft airbrushed halos around the structures, magenta tinting or magenta reflections on the
artwork, a graded or clouded backdrop, artwork filling the upper half of the frame, visible tiling seams, and
mirrored edge masses.
