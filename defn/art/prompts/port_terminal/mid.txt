Create the midground layer of a modular tiling background set for DEFN, a contemporary-to-near-future military
belt-scroller. This image is not a scene. It is one horizontal band of stacked shipping containers standing on
a quay, drawn against a completely flat, uniform, fully saturated pure magenta field (RGB 255, 0, 255) which
fills every part of the frame the containers do not occupy. The magenta is a cut-out backdrop that will be
removed automatically and replaced by separately drawn sky and distance layers; it is not sky and must never be
shaded, graded, textured, lightened, darkened, or blended into.

No reference image is attached, deliberately. Earlier layers in this set were contaminated by their references
— a sky drew the apron it was shown, a distant band drew the containers it was shown — so this one is
described in words and must follow the DEFN house style from that description alone.

This band is drawn at roughly half the height of the game screen. It is a near object standing a few metres
behind the characters, not a distant backdrop, and it is the second most prominent thing in the frame after the
characters themselves. Draw it with the confidence and weight that deserves.

Draw it as authored 2D digital cartoon illustration: clean dark hand-inked contours, crisp graphic silhouettes,
broad flat local-color regions, restrained two- or three-value cel shading, sparse purposeful texture, and
gently irregular hand-drawn edges. Weight the linework as a foreground subject: the outer silhouette of each
stack and the major overlaps where one container crosses another are the strongest, darkest lines in the image,
roughly two to three times the weight of interior marks. Interior lines are clean and deliberate, never thin,
faint, or sketchy. Every line must clarify volume, material, damage, or overlap.

Give each container real, authored detail, kept large and readable rather than dense:

  * Corrugated side walls read as a few grouped vertical ribs with clear shadowed valleys, not as many fine
    parallel lines. Group them into rhythms and let the grouping vary between containers.
  * Door ends carry chunky hardware: two tall door panels, heavy vertical lock bars, thick hinge blocks, and a
    simple handle. These are solid, slightly oversized shapes, never delicate mechanisms.
  * Every container has bold corner castings at its top and bottom corners, and a visible top rail and bottom
    rail, so the boxes read as engineered objects that stack.
  * Distress is authored and sparse: a handful of large readable dents that deform the silhouette, one or two
    patched repaint rectangles in a slightly different tone, rust running in broad streaks from a few corner
    castings, and a couple of scraped areas showing bare metal. No procedural grunge, no uniform edge wear, no
    fine scratches, no speckled noise.

Shade each container with a flat base colour, one clear shadow tone on the recessed door end and under every
overhang where a stacked container sits on the one below, and one restrained highlight along the top rail. Let
the shadow tones be strong enough to separate the boxes from one another; adjacent containers must never merge
into one mass.

Compose the band as a continuous run of stacks seen straight on from the side, with no perspective convergence
and no vanishing point. Vary it: stacks two to four containers high, some single containers standing alone,
several containers turned so their door ends face the viewer among the many showing their long sides, narrow
gaps between some stacks and none between others, and an irregular, interesting top edge. Vary the colours
across the run. Do not create one dominant landmark, one tallest tower, or one focal feature anywhere; the run
must be evenly interesting across its whole width so that no part of it announces itself when the band
repeats.

Every stack group in the image must be unique. Do not repeat an arrangement, a silhouette, a colour sequence,
or a distinctive container anywhere across the width, and do not build the run out of a repeating pattern of
any kind. The seamlessness asked for below is a property of the two outer edges continuing into one another,
and must never be achieved by making the interior periodic: a run whose left half recurs in its right half
puts the same stack on screen twice at once, which is far more obvious than any edge seam.

The container run occupies only the **lower half** of the frame. The entire upper half of the image is flat
magenta, empty of artwork, edge to edge, and the tallest stack reaches no higher than the vertical midpoint of
the frame. This proportion matters as much as the drawing: the band is stretched to its final height in the
game, and a run drawn tall in the canvas becomes a narrow tile that visibly repeats.

The bottom edge of the band is solid, straight, and fully opaque across the entire width, where the containers
meet the quay.

Every silhouette edge is a hard cel edge meeting the magenta directly, with the same clean dark contour the
containers carry elsewhere. There is no fade, no blur, no gradient, no glow, no haze, no atmospheric blending,
and no semi-transparency anywhere along that boundary. The magenta field stays perfectly flat and uniform at
RGB 255 0 255 everywhere it appears.

Light the band with flat, even, diffuse light that is identical at the left edge, the centre, and the right
edge. There is no directional sun, no raking light, no large cast shadow crossing the band, and no lateral
brightness gradient whatever. Shading is local to an individual container. A strip lit from one side cannot be
made to tile, because its two ends can never be reconciled.

Use a working-port palette: oxide red, rust orange, weathered olive-drab, dull maritime blue, faded seafoam
green, bone grey, and gunmetal container bodies, with warm charcoal and deep slate contours and shadows. Keep
the palette muted enough that the run reads as a mass rather than as a colour chart, and keep every colour
clearly distinct from magenta: use no pink, violet, purple, fuchsia, or hot red-violet anywhere in the artwork.

Make the band perfectly seamless from left to right for continuous horizontal scrolling. The stack rhythm,
top-edge silhouette logic, colour distribution, and every shape crossing one boundary must continue naturally
at the opposite boundary, and the bottom edge stays at one constant height across both boundaries. Keep roughly
the outer 10% on both sides compositionally plain, with no tall isolated stack and no high-contrast feature.
Construct a true continuous wraparound rather than placing matching stacks at both edges; the result must stay
convincing when repeated four times in a row.

Deliver only the container band and the magenta field: no ground, no apron, no quay surface below the
containers, no sky, no clouds, no horizon, no cranes, no vehicles, no people, and no gameplay props.

The containers carry no writing of any kind. Do not draw numerals, digits, letters, words, shipping-line names,
container codes, placards, logos, symbols, arrows, or any mark that resembles a written character, in any
orientation, anywhere in the frame, including worn or partially faded examples. Container ends carry only plain
panel divisions, ribs, hinges, lock bars, and handles.

Avoid photorealism, realistic 3D rendering, PBR materials, photographic metal texture, aerial or isometric
perspective, perspective convergence, lens distortion, depth of field, bloom, lens flare, film grain, painterly
concept-art brushwork, sketchy lines, thin or faint contours, noisy hatching, dense rivet or corrugation
detail, semi-transparent or glowing edges, soft airbrushed halos around the containers, magenta tinting or
magenta reflections on the artwork, a graded or clouded backdrop, artwork in the upper half of the frame,
visible tiling seams, and mirrored edge stacks.
