Create the ground layer of a modular tiling background set for DEFN, a contemporary-to-near-future military
belt-scroller. This image is not a scene and contains no sky, no horizon, and no buildings. It is one
horizontal strip of open ground, drawn edge to edge and top to bottom, which the game composites beneath
separately drawn distance layers. Depict the worn dirt-and-gravel hardstanding of a working container port
terminal, seen from a gently elevated angle looking down and across it.

No reference image is attached, deliberately. Earlier layers in this set were contaminated by the references
they were shown — a sky drew the apron it was given as a palette reference, a distant band drew the containers
it was given — and an earlier version of this ground, shown a snow-and-meadow background as an example of
organic ground language, took its cool whites along with the lesson. The drawing style below is the whole
brief and must be followed from this description alone.

Draw it as authored 2D digital cartoon illustration in the DEFN house style: clean dark hand-inked contours,
broad flat local-color regions, restrained two- or three-value cel shading, sparse purposeful texture, and
gently irregular hand-drawn edges. Use finer linework than a character sprite. Communicate material through
designed shapes rather than photographic surface texture.

Build the surface almost entirely from soft, organic, irregular shapes, and keep straight lines out of it.
This is the most important instruction in this prompt. The surface is compacted earth and gravel that has been
driven over for years, not a paved slab floor: broad drifts of pale dust, patches of darker damp ground with
soft wandering edges, wide beds of loose gravel that thin gradually into the dirt around them, faint oil
staining, and a few shallow scuffed hollows. Every boundary between these areas is a soft irregular curve.
There are no expansion joints, no slab edges, no paving grid, no kerbs, no painted lane markings, no straight
bars, no long straight edges of any kind, and nothing that resolves into a repeating geometric pattern. Tyre
tracks may appear but only as soft curving arcs, never as straight parallel lines.

Express the elevated viewing angle through vertical foreshortening, never through converging lines, and
express it through the *size and density of these organic features* rather than through any drawn line. The
patches, gravel beds and dust drifts are largest and most separated along the bottom edge, and become
progressively smaller, flatter, and more closely spaced toward the top edge, so the surface reads as a floor
receding away rather than as a wall standing up. Nothing converges, nothing fans, and there is no vanishing
point anywhere in the image.

Because the top of the frame is further away, let contour weight, contrast, feature size, and colour
saturation all fall off toward the top edge, and let the ground lighten a little with distance. This
top-to-bottom change is wanted. A left-to-right change is not, and is covered below.

The middle third of the image height, from roughly 32% to 65% down the frame, is where units will stand, and
it must be the flattest, quietest, and most open part of the surface: broad and simple there, with the
strongest patches kept toward the bottom and top edges.

Light the ground with flat, even, diffuse overhead light that is identical at the left edge, the centre, and
the right edge. The image must have no lateral brightness gradient, no directional sun, no raking light, no
large cast shadow, and no bright or dark region occupying one side of the frame. Any shading is local to an
individual patch. This matters more than atmosphere: a strip lit from one side cannot be made to tile, because
its two ends can never be reconciled.

This is an arid, hard-used industrial yard, not a meadow, a verge, or a field. Keep the palette dry and warm:
pale ochre and warm dust-grey compacted earth, cool blue-grey gravel beds, patches of darker damp brown-grey,
restrained rust-orange staining, and warm charcoal and slate contours. Vegetation is almost absent — at most a
very few small, dry, bleached straw-and-khaki weed tufts, widely separated, always the same dusty tone as the
ground they grow from. Nothing on this surface is green, and there is no grass, moss, meadow, foliage, leaf
litter, snow, frost, ice, or pure white anywhere in the image. Keep the surface value light enough that dark
unit silhouettes will read clearly standing on it, and keep the whole surface low in contrast so nothing on it
competes with the units.

Make the image perfectly seamless from left to right for continuous horizontal scrolling. The patch rhythm,
gravel distribution, lighting, and every shape crossing one boundary must continue naturally at the opposite
boundary. Vary the size and spacing of the patches so no rhythm is regular, and keep roughly the outer 10% on
both sides free of any unique or high-contrast feature. Construct a true continuous wraparound rather than
placing matching marks at both edges; the result must stay convincing when repeated four times in a row.

Every part of the surface must be unique. Do not repeat a patch arrangement, a gravel bed shape, or a
distinctive mark anywhere across the width, and do not build the surface out of a repeating pattern of any
kind. The seamlessness asked for above is a property of the two outer edges continuing into one another, and
must never be achieved by making the interior periodic: a surface whose left half recurs in its right half
puts the same marks on screen twice at once, which is far more obvious than any edge seam.

Deliver only the full-bleed ground surface: no sky, no horizon line, no buildings, cranes, containers,
fences, bollards, light masts, vehicles, people, animals, or gameplay props.

The ground carries no writing of any kind. Do not draw numerals, digits, letters, words, painted identifiers,
signage, arrows, symbols, or any mark that resembles a written character, in any orientation, anywhere in the
frame.

Avoid photorealism, realistic 3D rendering, PBR materials, photographic dirt or gravel texture, aerial or
isometric perspective, converging lines, a vanishing point, paving, slabs, concrete panels, expansion joints,
a tiled or checkerboard floor, painted road markings, straight edges, green vegetation, grass, moss, snow,
frost, lens distortion, depth of field, bloom, lens flare, film grain, painterly concept-art brushwork,
sketchy lines, noisy hatching, uniform grunge, dense pebble detail covering the whole surface, deep ruts,
potholes, puddles with hard edges, rubble, debris, visible tiling seams, and mirrored edge features.
