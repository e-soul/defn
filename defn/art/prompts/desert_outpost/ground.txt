Create the ground layer of a modular tiling background set for DEFN, a contemporary-to-near-future military
belt-scroller. This image is not a scene and contains no sky, no horizon, and no structures. It is one
horizontal strip of open ground, drawn edge to edge and top to bottom, which the game composites beneath
separately drawn distance layers. Depict a wide, worn **dirt road** crossing an arid desert basin -- the
packed, dusty track that leads out to a long-abandoned outpost, seen from a gently elevated angle looking down
and across it.

The most important quality of this image is that it is **quiet**. Characters fight on this surface, and rocks
and ruins are drawn standing on it by another layer, so it is a backdrop for them and never competes. It is a
smooth, soft, low-contrast, softly graded surface with very little on it. An earlier version was built from
cracked clay plates, gravel beds and scattered stones, each with its own contour; it was handsome on its own
and quite wrong here -- busy, restless, and embossed, so the whole floor read as a relief carving rather than
as ground that has been driven over for years.

So: **draw almost nothing on it.** The surface is packed dust and fine dirt. Its variation is a matter of
gentle changes of tone across broad, soft, ill-defined areas -- a slightly paler band where the dust has been
blown smooth, a faintly warmer area where the earth is redder, a barely darker stretch where the ground is
firmer underfoot. These areas have no edges: each one fades into its neighbour over a long, soft transition.
Two long, very shallow wheel-worn depressions may wander across the width as slightly darker, entirely
soft-edged bands, so the road reads as a road -- but they are suggestions, not ruts, and they never become
parallel lines.

There are no contour lines anywhere on this surface. Nothing is outlined. This is the one layer of the set
that is drawn without the house style's dark hand-inked contours, because every outline on a floor becomes a
raised edge and the floor starts to look embossed. There are no cracked plates, no crazing, no polygon
patterning, no gravel beds, no drifts with drawn crests, no pebbles, no clumps, no debris, no tyre tread
marks, no footprints, no potholes, no ruts with defined edges, and no hard-edged shapes of any kind. If a mark
would need a line around it, it does not belong here.

Keep the contrast very low across the whole image. The difference between the lightest and darkest part of the
surface is small -- this is a bright, dusty, evenly lit road, not a dramatic one. Nothing on it is dark enough
to read as an object, a hole or a shadow. A dark unit silhouette standing anywhere on this surface must read
instantly and cleanly against it.

Express the elevated viewing angle through vertical foreshortening, never through converging lines, and
express it only through the *scale of these soft tonal areas*: they are largest and loosest along the bottom
edge and become gradually smaller, flatter and more compressed toward the top edge, so the surface reads as a
floor receding away rather than as a wall standing up. Nothing converges, nothing fans, and there is no
vanishing point anywhere in the image. Let the ground also lighten very slightly and lose a little saturation
toward the top edge, since that part is further away.

The middle third of the image height, from roughly 32% to 65% down the frame, is where units will stand, and
it is the quietest, smoothest and most open part of an already quiet surface. The upper part of the frame,
which is where rocks and ruins will be drawn standing on this ground, must be smooth and even too, so that
those objects sit cleanly on it -- keep whatever little variation there is toward the bottom of the frame.

Light the ground with flat, even, diffuse overhead light that is identical at the left edge, the centre, and
the right edge. The image must have no lateral brightness gradient, no directional sun, no raking light, no
cast shadow, and no bright or dark region occupying one side of the frame. This matters more than atmosphere:
a strip lit from one side cannot be made to tile, because its two ends can never be reconciled.

This is a hot, arid desert basin, not a meadow, a verge, a field, or a beach. Keep the palette dry, warm and
narrow: pale amber and ochre dust, warm sand-tan, a soft coral-tinted warm grey where the earth is redder, and
a gentle dust-grey where it is more compacted. Nothing on this surface is green, and there is no grass, moss,
meadow, foliage, snow, frost, ice, standing water, or pure white anywhere in the image. No vegetation at all.

Build the wrap into the composition rather than hoping for it. The left-most 4% and the right-most 4% of the
frame are the same quiet, plain, evenly toned open dust: same value, same colour, with nothing at all crossing
either boundary, and the two margins interchangeable with one another.

Hold the overall brightness constant across the width as well. Any tall narrow vertical slice of this image,
taken anywhere across it, must average to the same value and the same colour as any other. There is no
vignette, no corner darkening, no brighter middle, and no drift from one side to the other, however gradual. A
gradual drift across the width becomes a hard vertical step where the two ends meet.

Make the image perfectly seamless from left to right for continuous horizontal scrolling. The tonal rhythm,
the lighting, and every soft area crossing one boundary must continue naturally at the opposite boundary.
Construct a true continuous wraparound rather than placing matching marks at both edges; the result must stay
convincing when repeated four times in a row.

Every part of the surface must be unique. Do not repeat a tonal arrangement anywhere across the width, and do
not build the surface out of a repeating pattern of any kind. The seamlessness asked for above is a property of
the two outer edges continuing into one another, and must never be achieved by making the interior periodic.

Deliver only the full-bleed road surface: no sky, no horizon line, no verges, no roadside, no kerbs, no
mesas, no boulders, no rocks, no ruins, no walls, no fences, no posts, no vehicles, no people, no animals, and
no gameplay props.

The ground carries no writing of any kind. Do not draw numerals, digits, letters, words, painted identifiers,
lane markings, signage, arrows, symbols, or any mark that resembles a written character, in any orientation,
anywhere in the frame.

Avoid photorealism, realistic 3D rendering, PBR materials, photographic sand or gravel texture, aerial or
isometric perspective, converging lines, a vanishing point, asphalt, paving, slabs, concrete, expansion
joints, a tiled floor, painted road markings, straight edges, contour lines, outlined shapes, embossing, a
relief or carved appearance, cracked or crazed earth, dried mud plates, gravel, pebbles, stones, debris,
rubble, craters, potholes, deep ruts, tyre tread patterns, footprints, green vegetation, grass, cacti, snow,
lens distortion, depth of field, bloom, lens flare, film grain, painterly concept-art brushwork, sketchy
lines, noisy hatching, uniform grunge, busy surface detail, high contrast, visible tiling seams, and mirrored
edge features.
